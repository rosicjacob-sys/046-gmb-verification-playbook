§1Executive summary — why your team is stuck right now

The misdiagnosis: "phone verification is the main issue." As of July 2025 Google removed user control over the verification method, and in 2026 most new profiles get video-verification-only. You need a working plan for every method — plus tactics to steer profiles toward phone/email when eligible.
  1. Google changed the game twice. (a) Since July 2025 you cannot choose your verification method — Google's risk engine decides per profile from account age, business type/category, region, public data about the business, and recent edits. (b) In 2026 most new profiles get video-verification-only. For many listings the phone option simply will not appear anymore.
  2. Your OTP numbers were silently rejected by Google. When "verify by phone" IS offered, Google delivers a code by SMS or automated voice call to the number on the profile (you type it into the GBP UI — no keypad needed). Zero codes from textverified.com US numbers = those pooled VoIP numbers were already flagged/reused. Google limits how many times any phone number may be used for verification across its services, and shared-pool numbers get burned fast. Rule: cheap shared pool = lottery; real-SIM-grade numbers = consistent.
  3. The market shifted under you in late 2025: SMS-Activate (the default tool for years) shut down Dec 2025; its infrastructure was bought by HeroSMS. 5SIM is still up but runs shared pools with known reliability issues on strict platforms like Google. Numbers that reliably work now: non-VoIP / real-SIM inventory (SMSPool, TextVerified's non-VoIP line, iDenfy, higher-priced "Google" slots) — or your own prepaid SIMs.
  4. Suspension wave since ~Sept 2025. Practitioners with 1,000+ profiles report 1–3 suspensions per week even for freshly video-verified profiles. In the wellness/peptide vertical expect more (§11). Appeals with proper documentation are reinstating well. Your old dormant accounts are an asset — but only if warmed up and isolated, because one restricted account suspends everything it manages.
New operating model: treat verification as a funnel, not a blocker. Before anything else — step zero: the metro must have (a) at least one owned number provisioned (§4 step zero; most teams start with *zero* US numbers — that's procurement, not research) and (b) an assigned filming plan for any video path (§5 remote operations). For each profile: (1) maximize the chance Google offers phone/email by building a "boring, real-looking" submission (§2); (2) have 2–3 phone-number tiers ready — owned SIM > non-VoIP rental > shared pool (§4); (3) if only video is offered, run the §5 production line so it passes first or second try; (4) everything through warmed accounts on isolated US residential IPs + anti-detect profiles (§6); (5) expect ~1–5% of output to need appeals and keep a standing evidence kit (§7).

Key facts that shape strategy:

  • Method is not user-selectable since July 2025 — *except* when a profile qualifies for other methods, in which case a "Change method" option appears in the UI. So "bypassing video" = making yourself eligible until Change-method shows up, then switching to phone/email/postcard.
  • Video requirements (Google's stated checklist): (1) current location context — nearby street, building signage, or surrounding businesses; (2) business equipment — office fixtures, furniture, displays; (3) proof of management/operations — business license, storage room, point-of-sale system, etc. Selfie is part of the flow to tie a human to the operation (§5).

    • Decision tree — run this for every profile

    1. Open GBP dashboard with the warmed account (§6). If a verification prompt is showing: read which methods are offered (phone / email / video / postcard / "Change method" button?).
    2. Phone or email offered → take it. Immediately pull a fresh number from tier T2/T3 of your phone pool (§4), put it on the profile, request code, log the attempt in docs/PHONE_LEDGER.md. Two failed numbers → escalate to owned SIM before spending more credits.
    3. Only video offered → don't fight it. Execute §5 and submit. While waiting (hours–days), keep the account active with light normal behavior; do NOT open a second profile from the same account in parallel.
    4. Video denied → re-upload (allowed). Change one variable at a time between attempts: different location framing, better signage visibility, clearer selfie, different time of day. Two strikes on video → escalate to postcard if offered / submit support request via Help Center chat asking for alternate method.
    5. "Verification pending" stuck >72h (a known state, reported in Google's own community) → do not edit anything; file a Help Center case referencing the profile ID; check back daily.
    6. No methods shown at all / errors on submit → almost always an account-health or IP problem, not a listing problem: re-run warm-up checks (§6), try again next day from same IP.

    Tactics to steer profiles toward phone/email (the "bypass video" kit)

    Practitioner heuristics — no single one is guaranteed, but the stack works better than any piece alone. The documented pre-claim trust stack for your vertical is in §11c.

    • Build a boring profile before requesting verification: 100% complete fields (description, hours, attributes, services), no keyword-stuffed name/category, business name exactly matching what exists off-site. Profiles that "follow Google's Guidelines" verify and survive at higher rates.
    • Have the phone number on the listing from the first save — a real mobile-grade number (not VoIP) raises the chance phone verification is offered *and* eligible for Change-method later.
    • NAP consistency offline before creation: 2–3 citations/directories with identical name-address-phone, ideally one of them older than ~2 weeks. Instant/phone paths lean on "what Google already knows."
    • Match the geography everywhere: US residential IP ≈ business state/city; phone area code ≈ business city (same region at minimum); account's default search region and Maps activity in that metro before creating (§6).
    • Use a real website domain for the profile link where possible (even a cheap parked/aged one beats none); home-based/service-area businesses without websites get routed to video more often.
    • Timing & pacing: create during local business hours of the target city; max 1 new profile per account every 2–4 days early in warm-up, scaling later; never batch-create from one account/IP in a day.
    • If you must recreate: deleting and recreating a listing re-triggers method assignment — only do this once, with an improved profile (better number, more citations), because repeated create/delete looks spammy.

§3Why your OTP numbers produced zero codes (diagnosis)

"Verify by phone" = Google sends a one-time code to the exact number listed on the profile, by SMS or automated voice call; you enter it in the GBP browser UI. No keypad/DTMF interaction is needed — so a pure SMS-receiving service can work, provided:

  1. The number isn't already flagged (used recently for Google verifications by someone else, or burned as VoIP spam), and
  2. You're actually receiving on that specific number in the window Google uses.

Google caps how many times any phone number can be used for verification across its services — so every pooled provider's inventory degrades constantly. The reason "most other services only offer Philippine numbers" is exactly this: PH/cheap numbers are heavily recycled; US real-SIM slots cost more, which is why cheap tiers never allocate them to you by default.

textverified specifically has documented practitioner complaints of flagged US numbers + no refunds (r/blackhatseo review thread). Don't build the pipeline on it alone again — demote it to backup tier (§4).

§4The phone-number pipeline that works in late-2026

Step zero — you have zero US numbers (the team's actual starting point)

Everything below assumes number procurement is already solved. If the team literally has no US/CA number able to receive texts, fix it on day 1–2 with a ~$30–40 standing kit — this is logistics, not research:

  • Hushed (hushed.com): $6.99/mo unlimited or $7.99 prepaid (30/90/365-day plans); US, CA and UK numbers across 300+ area codes; the number lands in the app within minutes and receives calls/texts from any sender — Google's verification SMS arrives right there. No SIM, no shipping; pick a CA number for Canadian profiles.
  • Burner / 2ndLine: ~$5/mo per US number — same idea, second source so you're not single-vendor dependent.
  • TextNow / TextFree (free): free US + CA in-app numbers that receive SMS; backup tier 0 only — recycled pool means some are already flagged, keep 2–3 on standby.

These are dedicated real working numbers: unlike shared OTP pools (textverified/5SIM) they aren't being hammered by thousands of other users, so Google's per-number usage limits apply cleanly and the number stays reachable later for re-verification / "we may call or text your listed number" events. Put Hushed/Burner on the profile as the default listing number; keep T3 SIMs (table below) as the escalation + appeal-evidence tier. Log every number in docs/PHONE_LEDGER.md with area code → assigned metro.

Operating rules

  • Always buy the "Google" service in "United States" on these platforms — never generic/multi-service slots, which get recycled across everything (WhatsApp, Telegram…) and arrive pre-flagged.
  • Price is a quality signal (practitioner rule from BlackHatWorld): for Google specifically, if a country/service slot is priced well above the cheapest options, that's usually real-SIM inventory — pay it for US.
  • Expect 1–3 failed numbers per successful verification. Pre-batch: buy ~5 numbers before an attempt session. If 5–6 consecutive numbers fail to deliver, stop — close the browser profile and restart next day from a fresh anti-detect profile + fresh IP (per BHW guidance; continued hammering flags the account/IP harder).
  • If Google delivers by voice call instead of SMS on your number: you won't see it in an OTP dashboard. Mitigations: pick "text/SMS" whenever the UI offers delivery choice; or route to a T3 SIM you can actually watch (a spare phone in a cradle is a $0 solution for the team); some rental services offer call-forwarding — check per provider.
  • Number hygiene ledger (non-negotiable): every number used gets logged: number | tier/provider | date | profile ID | account | IP | result(code received? verified?). Never reuse a flagged number; area-code-match the business city when possible; one number to one profile at a time.
  • Google Voice / TextNow / free apps: fine for low-stakes Google-account verifications, but VoIP-classified and often pre-flagged for GBP — not reliable as a primary tier in 2026.

Recommended standing budget (scale to your volume)

  • 10–20 owned SIM lines on the cheapest acceptable plans (~$100–350/mo) = T3 backbone + appeal evidence line. (Your vertical at 300/mo: see scaled sizing in §11e — 75–100 US + 25–40 CA lines.)
  • $50–150/mo across HeroSMS/SMSPool/T2 providers for T1/T2 volume.
Result: that combination removes phone verification as a blocking issue entirely — worst case any profile consumes one T2 number or one owned SIM and still verifies same-day.

§5Video verification playbook (your default path in 2026 — make it a production line)

Google's stated requirements: (1) current location context (street, building signage, surrounding businesses), (2) business equipment (office fixtures, furniture, displays), (3) proof of management/operations (business license, storage room, POS system). Plus the selfie step. Review = up to 3–5 business days; some submissions auto-clear in hours. Denial → re-upload is allowed.

Remote operations — when nobody on your team is at that location (PH-based team, US/CA listings)

Video verification does not require you to be there; it requires footage from the location. Run it as a delegated production line:

  1. Client / business staff films it (default for client profiles). For clinics and other client businesses, send the owner/staff the filming script below during onboarding — the "verification kit" is part of your claim package. ~10 minutes on site; receive raw files over WhatsApp/Drive. At 300 listings/mo this one habit removes video as a blocker for most profiles.
  2. Hire a local shooter (no client access / unresponsive owner): TaskRabbit-style errand or photo gigs in the target city, Fiverr local photographers, or staff at a neighboring business — $20–50 per location. Same script + address + exact profile name; collect raw footage (1080p+, no edits).
  3. Batch by metro. Cluster the queue by city (same clustering as §6). One on-site pass per metro covers every nearby listing in a single drive-by/walk-through: amortizes cost and keeps geo signals coherent across the cluster.
  4. No physical storefront at all? Don't force video: structure the listing as service-area (hide address, list service areas) or anchor it to a real documented office/suite — pair with §3's trust stack so the risk engine prefers phone/email. If Google still demands video on an SAB, film from where operations actually happen via option 1 or 2.
  5. Last resort: verification-as-a-service — operators who handle the on-site visit for a fee; use when volume justifies, log cost per listing.
Filming script to send to whoever is on site (paste-ready):
We're verifying {Business Name} at {full address}. Please record short phone clips (~30–45 seconds each), daytime, natural light, landscape mode — no editing, music, captions or screen recording: (1) stand on the street facing the storefront and pan slowly so the sign reading exactly "{Business Name}" is clearly legible (must match the Google profile character-for-character); (2) show the entrance/door number plus neighboring businesses for context; (3) walk inside and show the work area — counter/reception, equipment, shelves, POS register (~10–20 seconds of real movement; an empty staged room gets denied); (4) if a business license, storage area or POS system is visible, include it (Google lists these as "proof of management"); (5) optional short selfie at the front. Send us the raw files — don't trim or add effects.
Rejection triggers to avoid: stitched/edited clips · illegible signage · filmed location that doesn't match the listed address · same face reused across unrelated businesses (one operator face per metro cluster; after two denials on a location, change face AND framing).

Shot list — brick-and-mortar / storefront

  1. Selfie (in-app): neutral face, plain background, good light, no filters/edges/AI cleanup. Same person for all profiles a given "operator" submits (reuse the same 2–3 faces across a cluster of related listings; don't randomize wildly).
  2. Exterior pass: start outside on the actual street — pan to show the storefront sign with the exact business name from the profile, then the neighboring businesses/street for context. Walk slowly, handheld phone, natural light, daytime matching the city's local time.
  3. Interior/equipment: walk in (or film inside) showing real fixtures — counter, shelves, tools, POS register, workbench. 10–20 seconds of genuine movement; a staged empty room is a common denial trigger.

Shot list — service-area / home-based business (the hard one most teams hit)

Film from where operations actually happen (home office, garage workshop, vehicle): selfie → pan the work area → show equipment relevant to the category (plumbing parts, tools, laptops for consulting, products on shelves) → hold up a sign with the exact profile name if there's no permanent signage. Consistency rules:

  • The video location must plausibly match the address on file — if you film from PH/elsewhere while the listing says Texas, that mismatch is a denial/suspension vector. Film on-location or use a local operator; if impossible, align the business structure (e.g., SAB with service areas and a real US home-office source).
  • Name on sign/license = profile name exactly (a "John C. Doe" vs "John Charles Doe" mismatch is a documented appeal-killer from the forums — standardize legal-style names across everything).

Production rules that raise first-pass pass rate

  • Phone camera, 1080p+, natural audio on; no music, no overlays, no screen recording of other footage.
  • One take per profile; 30–90 seconds total is plenty.
  • Submit during the target city's daytime (local time), from that same geo/IP cluster as the account.
  • After submit: don't edit the profile while it processes. If denied, re-upload with exactly one changed variable, ideally a different day.
  • Track in ledger: video ID | shots used | operator/face | IP | submit date | result. Two denials on same location+face → change face AND framing before third try.

§6Account pool & infrastructure (the black-hat layer that decides your suspension rate)

Context from the field: since ~Sept 2025 even clean new profiles get suspended within days, and operators with 1,000+ listings take 1–3/week as "normal tax." Your dormant account pool is therefore valuable but must be re-qualified first.

6a. Triage the existing accounts (this week)

For each Google account in the pool, log: creation age, last activity date, 2FA state (recovery email/phone reachable?), any "unusual activity" banners, whether it currently manages GBP listings (and their health), and which manager vs owner level it sits at. Bucket into:

  • A = clean & recent → use immediately after light warm-up.
  • B = dormant but intact → 2–3 week warm-up before first profile.
  • C = flagged/restricted → appeal or quarantine. Do NOT put new work under a restricted account, because an account-level restriction suspends every listing it manages and blocks new claims.

6b. Warm-up routine for B/C-adjacent accounts (14–21 days)

  • Week 1: sign in daily from the SAME US residential IP + dedicated anti-detect browser profile; 15–30 min of normal use — Gmail read/compose, Google Maps searches in the target metro, a few YouTube/News scrolls. No GBP dashboard visits yet if avoidable.
  • Week 2: add Maps pin-drop/review-browsing behavior in the exact city where profiles will be created; keep IP+browser identical; local-time usage.
  • Week 3+: first profile creation (low-risk category), then pace at 1 profile / 2–4 days early, scaling to daily only after 2–3 verified without incident. Move an account forward only when each stage "stays quiet" — there is no magic day count; behavior in the first week matters more than fingerprint cosmetics.

6c. Infrastructure rules (one-to-one everything)

  • 1 Google account = 1 anti-detect browser profile (GoLogin/Demask/AdsPower/Multilogin-class) with stable settings: timezone ≈ target city, English (US), matching screen size/locale. Never share a profile across accounts.
  • 1 account = 1 US residential IP, sticky for the account's life; mobile proxies > datacenter, and where budget allows rotate among several residential exits per metro rather than one shared exit. Area code/city of phone number should sit in the same region as the IP.
  • No VPN-for-everything habits: a PH office IP touching US GBP dashboards is a tell — all GBP work runs through the US exits.
  • Owner contact emails: prefer domain-based mailboxes over fresh Gmail where you control the domain (documented suspension-reduction tactic); keep 2FA recovery on each account reachable by your team.

6d. Pacing & clustering

  • Group listings by metro; run one IP+account cluster per metro so cross-signal checks look coherent.
  • Sensitive categories only on A-tier accounts with ≥1 verified profile already.
  • Weekly, not daily, bursts read more human: e.g., 3–5 new profiles/account/week at steady state.

§7Suspension & appeal operations (expect this volume; make it boring)

Field baseline: plan for ~1–5% of profiles to suspend, concentrated in the first days after verification and after edits. A large operator with 1,000+ listings reports 1–3/week as normal since Sept 2025; another says *almost every new profile* they created got suspended within a couple of days — and every appeal filed with documentation was reinstated. That asymmetry (suspensions easy for Google to issue, appeals winnable with evidence) is the strategic takeaway: keep an evidence kit standing. (Your vertical: 5–15%, §11a.)

7a. Standing appeal-evidence kit (per business)

Google's official appeal tool accepts as optional evidence: official business registration, business license, tax certificates, utility bills (electricity/phone/water/internet). Hard rules from Google + forums:

  • Name and address on every document must match the profile exactly (standardize a legal-style name across citations, docs, video signage, phone plan holder where possible — the "John C. Doe vs John Charles Doe" case is why mismatches kill appeals).
  • Appeals are one-shot per decision. If denied, you get an "additional review" path with *new* evidence — so save your strongest doc for round 2, don't dump everything weakly in round 1. (Also: reply to the denial email directly with new proof — storefront photos included.)
  • Never create a duplicate profile for the same business while appealing (instant policy strike).

7b. Appeal workflow

  1. Same day as suspension: snapshot the listing state (screenshots of map presence, reviews count, prior verification status) — evidence that it was live.
  2. Audit before submitting (Google's own instruction): what changed in the 7 days pre-suspension? Edits to name/category/address/URL are top triggers; revert anything suspicious *without* stacking changes — make one change per day post-reinstatement, spaced over several days (especially "spammy" industries: locksmiths, drug rehab, garage door, personal injury).
  3. Submit via the official appeals tool from the account that owns/manages the profile; attach 1–2 best-matching docs + a short plain-English explanation. Track status in the tool (Submitted/Approved/Not Approved) — it only emails one decision per selected profile.
  4. >10 suspended profiles → use the bulk reinstatement flow: the tool asks "Are you requesting bulk reinstatement for 10 or more suspended locations?" → attach a spreadsheet with every Business Profile ID + supporting docs (name/address on each doc exact-matched). One email covers it.
  5. Account-level restriction (different beast): if the *manager/owner account* is restricted, all its listings suspend and new claims are blocked — appeal via the My-account page first; lifting the account restriction usually un-suspends the profiles under it. This is why isolation (§6a) matters: one bad account should not drag down a clean cluster.
  6. Reinstated → 48h hands-off, then resume edits at one-per-day cadence. Official SLA to plan against: most reinstatement requests reviewed within ~2 weeks (Mar 2026 update).

7c. Soft vs hard suspension (official Mar 2026 framework)

  • Soft: profile remains live on Search/Maps, but you cannot edit it — must submit an appeal to make changes.
  • Hard: profile removed; full reinstatement form + evidence flow applies. If Google removes the owner's account, all locations under it fall with it (and reinstate together).

§8Monitoring, ledgers & daily ops

Ledger files: docs/ACCOUNT_LEDGER.md + docs/PHONE_LEDGER.md

  • Account row: account | tier(A/B/C) | anti-detect profile ID | IP city | owner email domain | created/last-active | profiles owned | status.
  • Phone row: number | tier(provider) | area code | date used | profile ID | account | result (code? verified?).

This turns "which accounts are unhealthy?" from a feeling into a 5-minute weekly check.

Weekly health checklist (30 min, fixed day of week)

  1. Every A/B account's GBP dashboard: suspension notices, "improvements needed" banners, verification-pending >72h, restricted-account emails?
  2. Cross-check ledger vs Google status; route anything new into the appeal workflow (§7).
  3. Spot-verify 2 random proxy exits (still US-residential) + 1 anti-detect profile's fingerprint stability.
  4. Phone pool inventory: ≥10 fresh T2 slots or SIM minutes left? Reorder before mid-session starvation.
  5. Spot-audit 2 verified listings on Maps: NAP/category intact, no duplicate-merge surprises (documented cases of profiles merging into neighbors).
March 2026 heads-up: Google now says it may occasionally contact businesses via automated call, text, or WhatsApp on the verified phone number. Keep T3 SIM lines paid/active and treat any such contact as a health signal — engaging normally is cheap insurance.

§11Vertical runbook — peptides & wellness clinics, ~300/mo, US + CA

11a. Why this vertical is different

  • Google treats hormone/TRT/GLP-1/IV-therapy/wellness as high-risk medical categories: video-by-default verification plus stricter review (Monday Digital, Jul 2026 names "some healthcare verticals" explicitly; SproutSage: hormone clinics suspend more often than dermatology/medspas). Plan a 5–15% suspension rate, not the 1–5% baseline.
  • Documented triggers in this exact space (SproutSage, Mar 2026): (1) cure/structure-function claims in the description, posts, or linked website — "TRT cures low testosterone", "Peptides rebuild muscle tissue" are cited examples; (2) missing/expired medical-director or clinic licensure → suspended as "Unverified Healthcare Provider"; (3) review-fraud patterns (spikes of 5-star from new accounts).
  • Official framework now (Google community guide, Mar 2026): soft suspension = profile stays live but uneditable until appeal; hard suspension = removed. Reinstatement SLA: "most requests within 2 weeks"; one reinstatement request per account; denied appeals can be re-opened by replying to the denial email with *new* evidence (storefront photos, license docs).

11b. Naming & listing hygiene for your multi-city sub-brands

  • Different name per city = correct (kills exact-duplicate detection). But keep the name field clean: brand + simple descriptor ("Lone Star Peptide Wellness"), never keyword-stuffed — no "BPC-157 / Semaglutide / Best in Austin" in the business name. Peptide names belong only in services/description fields.
  • Exact-match chain (the appeal life-line): profile name = website title/contact page = every citation = license docs = utility bill. One mismatch ("John C. Doe" pattern) kills reinstatements — standardize before you create, not after suspension.
  • Categories: one clean primary per brand (weight-loss-center / wellness-clinic family) + at most one secondary; don't rotate categories across the fleet; sensitive medical subcategories only on seasoned A-tier accounts.
  • Description copy: service-descriptive only, zero cure claims — and keep the linked website equally clean, Google reads it too.

11c. Pre-claim trust stack (this is what flips video → phone/email/instant)

Documented pattern (Monday Digital Jul 2026 "7 steps", citing Whitespark/Darren Shaw's repeated testing; prep ≈ 4–8 weeks per new footprint, no guarantees but materially shifts outcomes):

  1. Real website per brand — single-page OK for a one-off, but multi-location = dedicated page per address; NAP on homepage + contact page + every footer; LocalBusiness schema markup; domain-based email (info@brand.com), never Gmail as the owner identity.
  2. Verify that domain in Google Search Console and claim the GBP from the same Google account — this is what makes *instant* verification possible (scales with domain trust). Do GSC before anything else for every brand.
  3. 5–10 citations with exact-match NAP, at least one aged ≥2 weeks before you claim.
  4. Maps/web footprint in the metro: pin activity, brand mentions, consistent references — before the claim, from the matching IP cluster.
  5. A few genuine early reviews where available; never engineered spikes (fraud pattern = trigger).
  6. Claim from the warmed account (§6), same IP/timezone as steps 1–4, during target-city business hours.
  7. Wait for citation indexing before requesting verification — "the single most important factor"; a brand claimed 2 days after site launch almost always rolls video.

Then run the §2 decision tree: phone/email offered → number tiers (§4); video-only → §11d.

11d. Video verification with no one on the ground (at your volume)

  • Standing retainer with 3–5 local shooting vendors across your active metros (Upwork/Fiverr/TaskRabbit; ~$15–30 per 90-second video). Send them the §5 shot list as a fixed client brief + the exact profile name on signage. With no budget cap, make this same-day service — it converts "video-only" from blocker to commodity.
  • If clinic addresses are leased/virtual offices you can access: film inside via keyholder/property manager (cheapest per-video option; batch 10–20 shoots per site visit).
  • SAB-structured listings: one real US home-office source filmed once, reused across that cluster — but vary face + room per listing to avoid cross-links.
  • Postcard fallback for slow-burn listings where mail forwarding exists at the address (~2 weeks, zero production cost) — use deliberately as a pacing valve.

11e. Sizing everything for ~300/mo US+CA (no budget cap)

  • Phone numbers: assume 50–60% of claims get phone/email path → ~160–190 codes/month; at 1.3–1.7 number attempts per delivered code → ~250–320 fresh numbers/month. Standing pool: 75–100 owned US SIM lines (Mint/Visible) + 25–40 CA prepaid lines (Fido/Rogers; CA area codes on CA listings); top up with T2 non-VoIP rentals ($800–1,500/mo). Calibrate the exact mix from your week-2 pilot numbers.
  • Accounts: ~14 profiles/day steady state. At 3–4 creations/week per seasoned account → ~45–60 active A-tier accounts in creation phase + a rolling warm-up pipeline (your dormant pool, 14–21-day ramp) that replaces suspensions/churn continuously. Cluster by metro: one IP+account group per metro, so cross-signal checks read coherently.
  • Canada: CA residential proxies + CA numbers + provincial license docs in the evidence kit; playbook otherwise identical.
  • Appeals capacity: at 5–15% suspension rate on 300/mo → 15–45 suspensions/month → standing ~0.5 FTE appeals process: batch via bulk-reinstatement flow (≥10), one request per account, exact-matched docs first pass, license/medical-director proof as the round-2 "new evidence", 2-week official SLA as the planning number.
